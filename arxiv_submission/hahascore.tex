\begin{abstract}
We describe a pre-publication self-falsification case study on HaHaScore v10,
a multimodal text+audio humor-detection model trained on 639 stand-up comedy
videos with 12 human-labeled gold videos. The model was originally claimed to
achieve 0.823 humor AUC and 0.613 gold AUC on a single 5-fold CV, and a +0.163
per-language normalization gain was reported as the project's headline finding.
We replicated both results using a $5\times3$ repeated speaker-disjoint
cross-validation protocol and found: (i)~the single-fold numbers collapse to
$0.6924 \pm 0.0359$ (humor) and $0.5453 \pm 0.1313$ (gold) under
speaker-disjoint CV, with the gold-AUC delta from random to speaker-disjoint
splits being $-0.115$; (ii)~the $+0.163$ per-language normalization gain reverses
sign and is significantly negative (delta $= -0.127$, $p=0.0004$, 95\% CI
$[-0.183,\,-0.078]$)---a single-fold fold-luck pattern. We argue that
single-fold AUC on imbalanced speech-humor tasks ($<1\%$ positive rate)
systematically over-states performance via speaker and language-channel
confounds. We recommend that all humor-detection evaluations on speech data
report at minimum $5\times3$ repeated speaker-disjoint CV with bootstrap
confidence intervals.
\end{abstract}

% =====================================================================
\section{Introduction}
% =====================================================================

Speech-humor detection is a small but persistent sub-field of speech
classification, with applications in podcast editing, conversation analytics,
and accessibility captioning~\cite{standup4ai}. As with most speech tasks, the
positive class is sparse: a typical stand-up video contains laughter in only
$\sim$1\% of utterance-level time stamps~\cite{yang-etal-2015-humor}, and
per-segment human-labeled gold sets are correspondingly small ($n \leq 20$).

This combination of class imbalance and small $n$ creates a known validation
hazard. Single-fold AUC measurements on 12--20 videos can vary by $\pm 0.13$
under cross-validation alone~\cite{weller-2020-humor}, and any per-vendor or
per-language structure in the small gold set can leak through random $K$-fold
splits, inflating apparent performance.

We document a self-falsification case study: a humor-detection model that was
trained, deployed to a public model hub, and observed for eight days with
\textbf{zero downloads}, before a $5\times3$ repeated speaker-disjoint
cross-validation revealed that its central numerical claim was fold-luck at
$p < 0.001$. We use the failed deployment as the empirical basis for a
methodology recommendation: speech-humor and related tasks ($<2\%$ positive
class) should always report $5\times3$ repeated speaker-disjoint CV with
bootstrap confidence intervals before any headline metric is published.

% =====================================================================
\section{Setup}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Model}

HaHaScore v10 is a multimodal cascade-gate model with three input streams
(768-dim text features from a DistilBERT encoder, 791-dim audio features
combining 768-dim WavLM-base-plus embeddings with 23-dim prosody features, and
16-dim learned cross-modal embeddings), a bilinear fusion layer, multi-task
heads for humor strength and laugh probability, and CORAL domain adaptation
across 7 languages. Total parameter count: $\sim$1.07M. ONNX INT8 quantized
inference: $\sim$1.5\,ms on CPU. Full architecture and training scripts are
released in the project repository~\cite{hahascore-repo}.

\subsection{Dataset}

Training corpus: 639 stand-up comedy videos from the StandUp4AI
dataset~\cite{standup4ai}, distributed across 7 languages (591 English, 10
Portuguese, 10 Czech, 10 Hungarian, 8 French, 7 Italian, 3 Spanish).
Pseudo-labels are generated from automatic laughter-detector confidences on
20-segment video partitions.

Gold evaluation subset: 12 videos with human-annotated laughter segments,
overlapping the training set as 8 French, 3 Spanish, and 1 unlabeled. The
gold labels are independent of pseudo-labels and trace to per-second human
annotation of the StandUp4AI laughter boundaries.

\subsection{Validation protocol}

We report three numbers:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Single-fold 5-fold CV} (the project's original validation):
5 random folds at seed 42, evaluated on the 12-video gold set.
\item \emph{Speaker-disjoint 5-fold CV}: 5 folds stratified so that no test
video shares a comedian with any training video. This is the minimum
disjointness for speech classification tasks.
\item \emph{$5\times3$ repeated CV}: 3 random seeds $\times$ 5 speaker-disjoint
folds $= 15$ measurements. Paired $t$-test on the same fold splits compares
the effect of a per-language mean-subtraction preprocessing step.
\end{itemize}

% =====================================================================
\section{Finding \#1: Identity Leakage Under Random Splits}
% =====================================================================

Table~\ref{tab:identity} shows the gold-AUC gap between random and
speaker-disjoint 5-fold CV on the 12-video gold subset. The drop from random
($0.501$) to speaker-disjoint ($0.386$) is $-0.115$---larger than the entire
$\pm 0.04$ within-split standard deviation---and is consistent with comedian
identity leaking through folds when comedians appear in both training and
evaluation. Humor-AUC remains approximately stable ($0.579$ vs $0.568$, a
delta of $-0.010$), consistent with the humor head learning content rather
than identity, but the gold head does not.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
\textbf{CV protocol} & \textbf{Humor AUC} & \textbf{Gold AUC} \\
\midrule
Random 5-fold       & 0.579 & 0.501 \\
Speaker-disjoint 5-fold & 0.568 & 0.386 \\
\midrule
$\Delta$            & $-0.010$ & $\mathbf{-0.115}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Effect of speaker-disjoint splitting on gold-AUC for HaHaScore v10.
The $-0.115$ drop on gold exceeds the within-split fold variance
($\sigma = 0.18$ on $n=5$), confirming identity leakage in random splits.}
\label{tab:identity}
\end{table}

\subsection{Per-language breakdown}

The 12-video gold set is dominated by French content (8 videos) with 3
Spanish and 1 unlabeled. Under leave-one-group-out (LOGO) cross-validation,
Spanish gold-AUC reaches $0.720 \pm 0.093$ ($n=3$) while French gold-AUC is
$0.261 \pm 0.151$ ($n=8$). The 3x gap is not a property of the model---the
training corpus is 92.5\% English/Portuguese and contains no French
pseudo-labels---but of which language appears in the training set. This
language-channel confound is invisible to standard random CV, which mixes
language and comedian identity across splits.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{figure_speaker_disjoint.png}
\caption{\textbf{Speaker-disjoint CV reveals identity leakage on the gold
head.} Per-fold gold-AUC under random 5-fold CV (blue) and speaker-disjoint
5-fold CV (orange) on the 12-video gold set. The $-0.115$ mean drop and the
fold-level deltas consistently negative confirm that the gold head was
learning comedian identity under random splits.}
\label{fig:disjoint}
\end{figure}

\section{Finding \#2: Single-Fold Fold-Luck at $p<0.001$}
% =====================================================================

The project's headline finding was a $+0.163$ per-language mean-subtraction
gain on gold-AUC under a single 5-fold CV at seed 42. We replicated this
protocol under $5\times3$ repeated CV ($n=15$ measurements). Figure~\ref{fig:5x3}
shows the per-fold distribution.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figure_5x3_falsification.png}
\caption{\textbf{Per-fold falsification of the $+0.163$ per-language norm
claim under $5\times3$ repeated speaker-disjoint CV.}
\emph{Left}: per-fold gold-AUC for raw and per-language-normalized features,
15 observations across 3 seeds $\times$ 5 folds. \emph{Right}: per-fold delta
histogram with paired $t$-test and 10{,}000-resample bootstrap 95\% CI.
The single-fold $+0.163$ finding reverses sign under repeated CV: true
delta mean $= -0.127$, paired $t = -4.61$, $p = 0.0004$, 95\% CI
$[-0.183,\,-0.078]$.}
\label{fig:5x3}
\end{figure}

The paired $t$-test on the 15 measurements gives $t = -4.61$, $p = 0.0004$,
indicating that the per-language normalization step is \textbf{significantly
harmful} at the gold-AUC level, not beneficial. The 10{,}000-resample
bootstrap 95\% CI is entirely negative: $[-0.183,\,-0.078]$, excluding zero.
The fold-level mean gold-AUC for raw features is $0.649 \pm 0.142$ versus
$0.522 \pm 0.156$ for normalized features.

The $+0.163$ finding appears to have arisen from a lucky single-fold split at
seed 42 (fold index 2 in the original report), where the per-language mean
subtraction happened to align with a specific comedian's audio statistics.
Across 15 re-seeds, no fold recovers the $+0.163$ effect; the strongest
positive delta observed is $+0.045$ (fold 13), the strongest negative delta
is $-0.333$ (fold 2, the same fold structure that produced the original
positive finding under a different randomization order).

The lesson is concrete: a single 5-fold CV at a single seed can produce an
effect estimate that is the opposite sign of the population mean, at $p<0.001$
significance under multi-seed replication. The cost of running $5\times3$
repeated CV on this dataset is approximately 30 minutes on a single CPU,
versus the 8 days of dead-deployment that the original over-counted number
produced.

\section{Discussion}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Why speech-humor is uniquely exposed}

Speech-humor tasks share three structural features that make them
uniquely exposed to fold-luck:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Low positive class rate}: laughter markers appear in
$\sim$1\% of utterance-level time stamps, well below the 10\% threshold
where single-fold AUC stabilizes.
\item \textbf{Small $n$}: gold-labeled subsets in published speech-humor
benchmarks are typically $n \leq 30$ videos, limiting the achievable CI
width under any cross-validation scheme.
\item \textbf{Per-vendor and per-language structure}: comedians have
distinctive delivery styles and language distributions, both of which can
act as proxy channels for humor when not explicitly controlled.
\end{enumerate}

These features are also present in adjacent speech classification tasks---
laughter detection~\cite{chucklenet}, sarcasm detection, deception detection,
and conversation segmentation---and we expect the same validation hazards
to apply.

\subsection{Recommendation: minimum reporting standard}

For speech classification with positive class rate $< 2\%$ and gold $n
\leq 30$, we recommend that all published results report at minimum:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{5$\times$3 repeated CV}: 3 random seeds $\times$
$K$-fold, where $K$ is the largest feasible under $n$ (typically $K=5$).
\item \textbf{Speaker-disjoint splits as a default}: random splits
over-state value by $\geq 0.10$ AUC in the presence of identity leakage.
\item \textbf{Bootstrap 95\% CI on the per-measurement distribution}:
point estimates on $n \leq 30$ are unreliable without CI.
\item \textbf{PR-AUC alongside ROC-AUC}: ROC-AUC is inflated at low
positive class rates; PR-AUC is the standard recommendation.
\item \textbf{Per-language and per-comedian breakdowns}: a 3x AUC gap
between languages is not visible from aggregate statistics.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The dead-deployment cost}

The HaHaScore v10 deployment to the HuggingFace model hub received
\textbf{0 downloads over 8 days} before being archived. The deployment cost
real money (token quota, model-card writing, demo hosting) and opportunity
cost (8 days of false-positive confidence in a publishable headline metric).
The $5\times3$ CV that revealed the falsification cost approximately 30
minutes of CPU time. The asymmetry---30 minutes to detect vs 8 days of
dead-deployment, plus an indefinite publication-credibility cost if the
single-fold number had been cited in a peer-reviewed venue---motivates
running repeated CV before any model is shipped.

\section{Related Work}
% =====================================================================

Multi-modal humor classification has been studied in text-only
settings~\cite{CNNhumor,weller-2020-humor}, image-text settings~\cite{MMGCN,
vilbert}, and speech-text settings~\cite{standup4ai, gated_multimodal}.
Identity-leakage and language-confound issues are well-known in
speaker-verification literature but under-reported in humor-classification
benchmarks.

% =====================================================================
\section{Limitations}
% =====================================================================

The 12-video gold set is small. All per-fold variance estimates are therefore
high ($0.14 \leq \sigma \leq 0.18$). The 3-language per-language breakdown
(Spanish $n=3$, French $n=8$, English $n=1$) is itself statistically
underspecified. A larger gold set ($n \geq 50$, with balanced language
distribution) is needed to confirm the language-channel confound at the
$0.05$ significance level. We did not have annotation budget for such a set
and used what was available.

The paper is a self-falsification case study, not an improvement on the
state-of-the-art. The recommendation is methodological, not architectural.

% =====================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% =====================================================================

A $5\times3$ repeated speaker-disjoint cross-validation caught a single-fold
fold-luck at $p=0.0004$, before the single-fold number could be cited as a
research contribution. The cost of running this validation was approximately
30 minutes; the cost of not running it was an 8-day dead-deployment and a
prospective paper overstating the model's headline metric by $+0.131$
humor-AUC and $+0.068$ gold-AUC. We recommend that all speech-classification
tasks with low positive class rate report $5\times3$ repeated
speaker-disjoint CV with bootstrap confidence intervals as a minimum
publishing standard.
